Why the categories matter

Specific distinctions that protect against inflated capability claims

Learning is not deployment

The AI courses documented in Singapore and Banjul are genuine AI capacity-building outputs. They do not establish that those centres build or operate AI applications. Similarly, York’s AI-enhanced demining training does not establish that York developed or owns the detector.

Partnerships have different roles

Shanghai’s Chinese Voice Think Tank collaboration is confirmed AI knowledge work. AI-related logos, broad university relationships and attendees at AI training are not equivalent to an AI implementation partnership.

Evidence rules

Interpreting the map conservatively

Documented

A concrete AI-related programme, activity, publication or partnership is explicitly reported. This does not automatically mean technical ownership or a deployed AI system.

Discussed

The presentation mentions AI as an issue, discussion topic, instructional illustration or exploratory collaboration without establishing delivery.

Planned

Future AI intention is described, but no completed activity is established. This category is never counted as current delivery.

Not evidenced

No relevant explicit evidence appears in the reviewed presentation. This is not the same as “none,” “incapable” or “not interested.”

Recommended centre-validation questionnaire

Collect uniformly from all centres before selecting network pilots or leaders.

01 • Actual AI use

Which AI tools, courses, products or services are currently used or delivered? Who operates and maintains them? Give one demonstrable example.

02 • People & expertise

Which staff or partner teams can teach, build, procure, evaluate or govern AI? What is their available capacity?

03 • Stakeholder demand

What AI-related questions and requests have governments, firms, schools or communities raised? Which needs remain unmet?

04 • Partners & data

Which partners would jointly deliver or fund AI capacity building? What data and permissions are available for pilots?

05 • Safeguards

What human oversight, privacy, accessibility and responsible-use practices are actually followed?

06 • Network contribution

Would the centre contribute case studies, learners, trainers, technical review, local partners or pilot deployment sites?
